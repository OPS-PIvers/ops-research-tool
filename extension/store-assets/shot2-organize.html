The March on Washington, 1963

On August 28, 1963, more than 250,000 people gathered on the National Mall in one of the largest political rallies for human rights in United States history. Organizers had spent months coordinating transportation, marshals, and a program of speakers drawn from labor, faith, and civil rights organizations. The day’s final address, delivered from the steps of the Lincoln Memorial, reframed the movement’s demands as a test of the nation’s founding promises.

In the months that followed, public pressure built toward the legislation that would become the Civil Rights Act of 1964, and organizers turned to sustaining momentum across the country.
